## Design Specificity Verdict

**Strongly authored, with one repetition problem.** This is recognizably Helm rather than interchangeable cybersecurity SaaS. The mineral-paper/deep-pine palette, architectural display type, sculptural helm artwork, ruled choices, restrained motion, published pricing, and candid limitations form a coherent “quiet security partner” position. Light and dark themes preserve that identity unusually well.

The weakness is internal sameness: Pricing, Resources, and Trust spend nearly the entire first viewport on the same secondary architectural hero. Their copy changes, but their composition does not. A pricing evaluator, a guide seeker, and a due-diligence reviewer have materially different intent; the repeated full-screen hero makes these routes feel like themed pages rather than purpose-designed decision surfaces.

**Deterministic scan:** one isolated detector pass returned 14 advisories across two files: 10 local font-size ramp deviations and four color-literal findings in `Resources.css` and `Trust.css`. The two Trust shadow-color findings are false positives because they exactly match the documented Surface Whisper shadow. The two Resources color literals are low-risk token drift. The typography deviations are real but currently form coherent local hierarchy rather than visible breakage. The stale `.impeccable/design.json` sidecar makes sidecar-derived token findings less authoritative than `DESIGN.md`.

**Visual overlays:** no reliable user-visible overlay is available. Mutable-injection preflight failed—neither the title mutation nor script marker persisted—so no live server was started and no overlay was claimed. Fresh-tab browser evidence at 390×844 found no horizontal overflow on Resources or Trust.

## Overall Impression

The site now feels calm, candid, and expensive in a way that belongs to Helm. The original error-state, mobile-drawer, Resources, short-phone hero, and product-motion problems are gone. The largest remaining opportunity is to let high-intent routes perform their job immediately instead of replaying the brand introduction before showing prices, guides, or trust evidence.

## What’s Working

1. **The visual world has a real point of view.** Palette, typography, imagery, hairlines, asymmetric forms, and restrained interaction communicate steadiness without cybersecurity clichés.
2. **Trust is built with specifics rather than borrowed prestige.** Published prices, vendor attribution, the two-operator disclosure, known gaps, and exact scan boundaries create emotional credibility.
3. **Resources now has excellent information architecture.** Four task-first choices turn a 25-guide library into a manageable decision, then native disclosures hide the remaining depth.

## Cognitive Load and Emotional Journey

The site fails two of eight cognitive-load checks: minimal choices and working-memory support. Desktop navigation exposes nine simultaneous controls once Auto, Light, and Dark are counted separately. Pricing asks visitors to retain price, term, minimum, fit, and scope across four unequal cards, with two offers nested inside Helm Aware. Resources is the strongest surface: four starting choices and four collapsed groups stay within working-memory limits.

Emotionally, the homepage moves cleanly from calm curiosity to concrete trust and low-pressure action. Resources creates relief and immediate recognition. Pricing’s valley is the full viewport between arriving with pricing intent and seeing a price. Trust’s valley is the long article after the excellent four-part summary. Manufacturing creates urgency but makes a novice decode regulation before understanding the decision.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] High-intent pages bury their actual task below a full viewport

**Why it matters:** Pricing shows no price in its first viewport; Resources shows no guide; Trust shows no glanceable summary. Visitors who already know what they need must consume a brand statement before completing the route’s job.

**Fix:** Keep the cinematic full-height hero on the homepage. Give Pricing, Resources, and Trust compact 55–65vh or split heroes that reveal the first price comparison, task choice, or Trust summary in the initial viewport.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

### [P1] Scan consent and next-step copy is treated as visual residue

**Why it matters:** The homepage’s most consequential explanation—leaving for the secure portal, report timing, email follow-up, and unsubscribe—is 12px at 54% alpha over imagery in light mode. Trust is most important immediately before submission.

**Fix:** Separate “What happens next” from consent. Use at least 14px text, guaranteed AA contrast, shorter line length, and a quiet solid or tinted backing surface. Keep the no-card/no-meeting reassurance adjacent to the action.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable typeset`

### [P2] Pricing explains each offer but does not support direct comparison

**Why it matters:** Visitors must remember price, term, minimum, best-fit case, and scope while moving among four large, unequal cards. Helm Aware’s two offers make the matrix asymmetric.

**Fix:** Add a compact ruled comparison ledger above the detailed cards: offer, best for, starting price, commitment, and minimum. Keep richer cards below.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] Manufacturing asks novices to decode compliance before acting

**Why it matters:** The first viewport combines the status change, SPRS, Affirming Official, DFARS reporting, 110 NIST requirements, audience, CTA, and pricing path.

**Fix:** Lead with one qualifying decision, then move “What changed / What did not” into a ruled block immediately below the hero.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`

### [P3] Navigation carries avoidable control noise and one misleading cue

**Why it matters:** Three always-visible theme buttons compete with Free scan, while internal industry links use the diagonal external-link arrow.

**Fix:** Collapse theme selection into one compact control or move expanded choices into the drawer/footer. Reserve the diagonal arrow for true external destinations.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan — first-time buyer:** Pricing makes Jordan scroll before seeing a number. Manufacturing assumes fluency in multiple compliance acronyms. The smallest copy contains the actual portal and follow-up expectations.

**Riley — deliberate stress tester:** Trust’s candid disclosure is excellent, but Riley will look for last-reviewed dates, remediation status, and evidence links. The recovery page is now a strength: it avoids technical leakage and offers multiple concrete paths.

**Casey — distracted mobile visitor:** Source and browser checks confirm persistent Free scan access, focus trapping, reduced-motion handling, and no 390px overflow on Resources/Trust. Residual risks are the dense consent and sub-44px scroll/theme controls.

**NJ owner/operator:** Published pricing, local language, no required meeting, and candid vendor attribution reduce skepticism. Repeated full-height heroes still slow the owner arriving with an urgent insurer, payment-fraud, or contract question.

## Minor Observations

- The error route has an empty document title even though its visible recovery content is strong.
- Scroll cues are 42px desktop and 36px at the general mobile breakpoint; drawer theme choices are 38px.
- The homepage does not visibly mark Home as the current navigation location.
- Resources disclosure semantics should be verified once with VoiceOver or NVDA.
- The first cold secondary-hero view can briefly appear blank before reveal content settles.
- Trust’s excellent four-part summary should sit closer to the hero.
- Detector-only drift remains in local Resources/Trust typography and two Resources color literals; it is not currently a visible defect.
