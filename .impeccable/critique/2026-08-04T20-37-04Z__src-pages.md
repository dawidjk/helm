## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for Helm, with template flattening beyond the homepage.** The mineral-paper, pine, and oak world; Tenor Sans and Manrope pairing; sculptural helm; candid proof; published pricing; and industry-specific motion are recognizably Helm. An unrelated SaaS company could not adopt the homepage unchanged. Specificity weakens on product, pricing, Resources, Trust, and Contact because the same secondary hero and band rhythm leave copy carrying most route-level distinction.

## Overall Impression

The site now feels calm, candid, and expensive in the right way. Its largest opportunity is to make the buying journey as composed as the visual system: guide visitors by problem, contain edge states within the same trust language, and remove the remaining neon-era motion vocabulary.

## What’s Working

- The two-image hierarchy and material palette establish a specific alternative to generic cybersecurity theater.
- Published prices, explicit scope, vendor attribution, and candid Trust content create credibility without fabricated proof.
- Core interaction foundations are sound: meaningful form validation, visible actions, 44px primary targets, reduced-motion handling, off-screen industry-motion pausing, and optimized/discoverable hero images.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The global error state abandons the production brand and accessibility contract

**Why it matters:** At the most fragile trust moment, visitors receive a full-screen Matrix animation, neon green, monospace type, a 50ms drawing loop, an unlabeled search field, and suppressed focus treatment. This contradicts both the site’s calm promise and WCAG expectations.

**Fix:** Replace it with a static Japandi recovery surface using shared tokens, a programmatic search label, visible focus, clear Home and Contact paths, and reduced-motion-safe behavior.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] The full-screen mobile drawer does not isolate keyboard focus

**Why it matters:** Scroll is locked, but obscured page controls remain tabbable behind the drawer. Keyboard and switch-control users can lose their place and interact with content they cannot see.

**Fix:** Add `aria-controls`, move focus into the drawer, mark the background inert, contain traversal while open, close on Escape, and restore focus to the burger.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

### [P1] Resources presents 25 equal-weight choices

**Why it matters:** An owner arriving with an urgent problem must scan a library instead of recognizing the next task. This creates three cognitive-load failures: chunking, minimal choices, and progressive disclosure.

**Fix:** Lead with four task-based starting points—protect email, stop payment fraud, answer an insurer/customer, and meet CMMC/HIPAA needs—then group or progressively reveal the full library.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape`

### [P1] Short mobile industry heroes can bury the primary action

**Why it matters:** The manufacturing chip, long headline, regulatory paragraph, CTA, pricing note, and scroll cue compete inside the first viewport, while late mobile CSS restores larger padding and type.

**Fix:** Add a final short-viewport/narrow-phone composition with tighter vertical rhythm, smaller headline bounds, and conditional scroll-cue removal. Verify at 375×667 and 390×844.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Continuous product motifs preserve the discarded neon-era language

**Why it matters:** Scanner, sonar, waveform, and pulse loops spend CPU off-screen and contradict the documented rule that motion belongs to the three industry heroes.

**Fix:** Freeze motifs by default, replace fixed mint/neon colors with pine/oak tokens, and use at most a one-time hover/focus response.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable quieter`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan — first-timer:** Product names require translation; Services is absent from the primary navigation; the CMMC hero introduces several acronyms at once; Resources provides no guided starting point.

**Riley — deliberate stress tester:** An unlisted consumer email provider can bypass the finite domain blocklist; route-family active states are incomplete; the theme control forces cycling; the runtime-error path breaks the documented system.

**Casey — distracted mobile user:** The industry CTA can fall below the fold on short phones; the mobile drawer’s focus behavior is unsafe; the 25-row resource list creates long-scroll fatigue.

**Pat — New Jersey owner/operator:** Pat arrives with an insurer form, suspicious payment, or deadline and needs “what should I do next?” Product names and pricing cards still require interpretation, although published pricing and candid Trust content reduce skepticism.

## Minor Observations

- Pricing explains arithmetic better than product fit; consistent “Best for…” lines would reduce translation effort.
- Product, FAQ, About, and Free Scan contain heading-level jumps that weaken screen-reader navigation.
- Superseded gradient/neon CSS remains underneath the production layer, enlarging the bundle and making regression easier.
- The quiz progress bar animates `width` instead of a transform.
- Privacy-choice buttons use a 42px target and an old bright-green action color instead of the current 44px/token standard.
- The homepage repeats the “about a minute” timing promise beside the same form.

## Questions to Consider

- Should navigation prioritize the visitor’s industry, or the problem they already know they have?
- Is Resources meant to be a library or a decision assistant?
- If motion belongs only to industry context, what strategic job are the pricing-card loops doing?
- Could a consistent “Best for…” line remove more pricing confusion than another explanatory section?
- What is the shortest honest CMMC first viewport that still gets a worried owner to the right action?
